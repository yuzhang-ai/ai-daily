**Publication date: 23 November 2026**  
**Coverage window: 16 November 2026, 00:00 to 22 November 2026, 23:59 (Beijing time)**

## 1. Lean Proof of the Riemann Hypothesis Certified by the Community, Author Explicitly States Working with Claude (11/20–11/21)

**What happened.** On 20 November, Levent Alpöge released an approximately 17-million-line Lean repository and wrote in the remark: “I believe I have, working with Claude, discovered a proof of the Riemann Hypothesis”; he also wrote that the method “easily extends” to the Generalized Riemann Hypothesis for Dirichlet L-functions. He chose to release the Lean formalization first rather than write a human-readable long paper first. [Official] [Author remark](https://alpo.ge/remark.pdf)

On 21 November at 13:55 UTC (21:55 Beijing time), the Lean and Mathlib communities issued a joint statement endorsed by the Lean FRO and the Mathlib Initiative: expressing full confidence in the correctness of the proof; confirming that the proven theorem agrees with the reviewed `RiemannHypothesis` statement in Mathlib; verifying that the repository compiles; and noting that the exported proof is accepted by the official Lean kernel as well as multiple independent checkers including con-leche and con-ron. [Official] [Lean community statement](https://leanprover-community.github.io/blog/posts/riemann-hypothesis/)

**Why it matters.** What truly must be separated are two things: whether the machine has confirmed that this proof holds in Lean, and whether humans have understood it and written down teachable ideas. The author himself stated that the argument is extremely long and that he submitted the complete formalization before fully mastering the vast surrounding mathematics. *Scientific American* quoted the community's exact phrase “full confidence… is correct” and noted Kevin Buzzard’s reminder: one must first check whether the theorem statement in Lean is indeed the Riemann Hypothesis itself. [Media] [Scientific American](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/the-ai-riemann-hypothesis-proof-is-almost-certainly-true-independent-mathematicians-attest/) Ongoing discussions emerged across technical communities. [Third party] [Hacker News author statement thread](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50574968) | [Hacker News community certification thread](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50589618) Discussion volume merely demonstrates that the topic exists, not audience statistics.

**Limitations.** Passing formal verification does not mean scientific understanding is complete, nor does it mean the Clay Institute’s million-dollar prize has been awarded—the latter typically still requires formal publication and peer review. Describing this event as “AI independently conquered a Millennium Prize Problem” goes further than the author’s original words. The MIT verification page recorded 175,187 modules compiling successfully and noted that some slower kernel replays were still ongoing at the time; however, the page explicitly states last updated 2026-11-22 16:20 UTC, corresponding to 23 November 00:20 Beijing time, which has passed this week’s window. Citing these module figures requires labeling the timestamp. [Third party] [MIT verification page](https://math.mit.edu/~drew/grh/) In addition, a dissenting article titled that the proof is invalid has been circulating; this text was not opened in this review, so it cannot be written on this basis that community certification has been overturned.

## 2. Anthropic Completes IPO: Priced at $83, Ticker ANT (11/18–11/20)

**What happened.** On 18 November, Anthropic announced the pricing of Class A at $83.00, expected to open on Nasdaq under ANT on 19 November and close on 20 November. [Official] [Pricing announcement](https://www.anthropic.com/news/anthropic-prices-initial-public-offering)

The 20 November closing announcement confirmed that the underwriters fully exercised their overallotment option. The IPO raised approximately $93.5 billion (before fees), of which approximately $91.6 billion went to the company. Concurrently, Class N shares were sold in a private placement to AMD, Broadcom, Google, Micron, Microsoft, NVIDIA, and TSMC Partners, also at $83 per share, totaling approximately $26.0 billion. The official release stated that the IPO (including shares sold by existing stockholders) and private placement combined totaled approximately $119.5 billion before fees. [Official] [Closing announcement](https://www.anthropic.com/news/ipo-close)

Media reported that it opened at $111.50 on Thursday and closed at $97.17, approximately 17% above the offer price; it fell 8.5% on Friday. [Media] [CNBC greenshoe and first day](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/20/anthropic-ipo-greenshoe.html) | [CNBC second day decline](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/20/anthropic-stock-ai-amd-nvidia.html)

**Why it matters.** This matters not because yet another company completed financing, but because after a lab turns into a public company, product cadence, safety commitments, and capital expenditures will be scrutinized simultaneously by quarterly earnings reports and share prices. [Inference]

**Limitations.** The official closing press release did not state a “$1.5 trillion valuation.” Market rumors or secondhand valuations cannot substitute for official figures. The first-day gain and second-day pullback are reported market reactions, not a conclusion on long-term valuation.

## 3. OpenAI Releases GPT-6.2 Astra and Sol: Official Price Cuts and Claims of Stronger Performance on Professional Tasks, While Community Disputes Stability (11/17–11/18)

**What happened.** The official learn page is dated 17 November 2026; the publication time on OpenAI News RSS was 18 November at 01:45 Beijing time, consistent with the evening of 17 November US Pacific Time. Astra is positioned as the new flagship for the hardest projects, and Sol as an efficient model for everyday work, rolling out to ChatGPT Work, Codex, and the API. [Official] [Release post](https://openai.com/index/introducing-gpt-6-2-astra-sol)

Astra’s API list price is $8 per million input tokens, $0.40 for cached input, and $40 for output. The official documentation states that input and output are 20% lower than GPT-6 Astra, and cached input is 60% lower. Sol maintains the unit price of GPT-6.1 Sol. Official evaluations state that Astra scores 5.2 points higher than its predecessor on Agents’ Last Exam at less than one-third the cost—these are vendor figures, not independent re-tests verified in this review. [Official]

The system card overview states: both models reached the Critical threshold in cybersecurity capabilities and the High threshold in Biological and Chemical capabilities; Astra reached the High threshold in AI Self-Improvement, while Sol did not reach that threshold. Only the overview was verified in this review, without reading through all appendices. [Official]

**Why it matters.** What the industry debates is often not another first-place spot on a leaderboard, but whether it stops early, takes shortcuts, or shows instability in long-horizon tasks. Related discussions can be found in [Third party] [Hacker News release thread](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50535230) | [Hacker News Simple Bench follow-up thread](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50588916). This is evidence that the discussion exists, not a statistic across the entire audience.

**Limitations.** Both benchmark leads and cost comparisons come from official configurations and cannot substitute for practitioners’ own regression testing. What the system card provides is a threshold determination under the Preparedness Framework, not an independently verified third-party security audit.

## 4. Google Releases Gemini 4 Neon (11/18)

**What happened.** Google released Gemini 4 Neon on 18 November, officially positioned to deliver “frontier-level intelligence with fast end-to-end task completion” across complex software engineering, knowledge work, and scientific workflows. Pricing is $3 per million input tokens, $0.15 for cached input (officially described as a 95% discount), and $15 for output, with a 1-million-token context window. It launched that day to Gemini API / AI Studio, Gemini for business, as well as the Antigravity and Gemini apps for paid Pro/Ultra subscribers. [Official] [Introducing Gemini 4 Neon](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/gemini-models/introducing-gemini-4-neon/)

Evaluations described in the official narrative include: scoring 61 on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index at the xhigh thinking level, ranking 3rd overall; and ranking 2nd on the Vals Index. These are vendor citations or official evaluation setups, not independent re-tests verified in this review. [Official] Discussions emerged in the technical community. [Third party] [Hacker News](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50552424) This review did not read through all comments, and the focal points of discussion are not represented as verified community consensus.

**Why it matters.** This was Google’s primary model release of the week. Cached pricing and the 1-million-token context window are explicitly stated by the official release; leaderboard rankings can only be treated as vendor claims. Whether it is worth switching models still depends on whether repository-level tasks and long-horizon agents hold up reliably in one's own workflow.

**Limitations.** Timelines must be distinguished: Gemini 4 Carbon, Hydrogen, and others were released on 10–13 November and fall outside this week’s window. Genie 4 expanded availability this week to Ultra subscribers and the Gemini API, but the model itself was announced in October and should not be conflated with Neon as a new world model debut.

## 5. Thinking Machines Releases Inkling 2 Mini: Open-Weight Looped Transformer Model, Locally Runnable, with Follow-up Replications (11/20)

**What happened.** On 20 November, Thinking Machines released Inkling 2 Mini and opened its weights. The official explanation states this is a looped transformer: middle layers run three times per token; total MoE parameters are 26.4B, with 3.4B active, and 5.4B weight activations per token when accounting for looping. The model card is also dated 20 November, licensed under Apache 2.0. The official description states native NVFP4, with a total memory footprint of approximately 20GB, capable of running on consumer laptops or desktops; it also notes that it can run via Ollama on a Mac with at least 32GB RAM, with inference support from vLLM, SGLang, and others. [Official] [Introducing Inkling 2 Mini](https://thinkingmachines.ai/news/inkling-2-mini/) | [Model card](https://thinkingmachines.ai/model-card/inkling-2-mini/)

Official benchmarks claim that among open-weight models with fewer than 100B total parameters, it leads on coding tasks such as DeepSWE v1.1; they also explicitly state that looping cannot increase knowledge storage, and factual capability generally still scales with total parameter size, needing search and tools to compensate. These scores reflect the company's own evaluation setups. [Official] The official demo also mentioned that researchers built a playable flight simulator offline on a MacBook Pro during a flight. This is a vendor narrative.

**Why it matters.** Architectural experimentation by small-to-medium labs, combined with reproducible local deployment, generated discussion beyond a single repost. [Third party] [Hacker News release thread](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50581260) | [Hacker News “5.4 GB Memory” follow-up thread](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50591853)

**Limitations.** “5.4 GB” is the headline phrasing of the community follow-up post, not the default memory figure in the official model card (which officially lists approximately 20GB, with a starting point of 32GB RAM for Mac). For practical deployment, memory should first be prepared according to official specifications, treating community compression experiments as follow-ups not documented in the model card rather than official commitments. Looped transformer architectures trade repeated computation for reasoning depth, but knowledge capacity remains constrained by total parameters.

An Alibaba Group press release on 16 November announced the official launch of Qwen4-Max: 2.4 trillion total parameters, 71 billion active per token (approximately 25% fewer than Qwen3.8-Max under the same 2.4T metric), native vision, and a 1-million-token context window. The price singled out in the press release is for Implicit Cache Hit input: $0.075 per million tokens, while stating that open weights for smaller Qwen4 models will be released soon. ### miHoYo Gamer: First Submitted on 14 November, Revised This Week, Incomplete Open Source

The paper *Gamer: A Generalist Vision-Language-Action Foundation Model for Long-Horizon Real-Time Game Agent*, authored by the miHoYo Honkai AI R&D Team, was published on arXiv:2611.18397. [Official — preprint] [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18397) The initial submission time was 2026-11-13 16:39:30 UTC, corresponding to 14 November at 00:39 Beijing time, prior to the start of this week’s window. Revisions occurred this week on 17 November and 18 November, with v3 marking the last revised date as 18 November 2026. The project page and code repository are now accessible; the repository creation time was not verified in this review. [Official] [Project page](https://mihoyo-gamer.github.io/) | [GitHub repository](https://github.com/RainBowLuoCS/Gamer)

The paper explicitly states: what is released is the inference engine and the checkpoints used for the CPT scaling study; it does not disclose *Honkai: Star Rail* data, nor does it release model weights trained or fine-tuned on that data. Therefore, it is inappropriate to describe this as a “fully open-source gaming agent.”

### Navier–Stokes Finite-Time Blow-Up: Currently Still an Author Claim, Key Certificates Pending Release

On 20 November, Levent Alpöge and Tristan Buckmaster released the paper *Finite-time blow-up for the Navier–Stokes equations*. The authors claim: unforced 3D incompressible Navier–Stokes equations exhibit a finite-time blow-up, thereby negating (A) and (B) in Fefferman’s statement of the Millennium Prize Problem and asserting (C) and (D). [Official — author] [GitHub paper and repository](https://github.com/tristanbuckmaster/navier-stokes/)

This is an author claim, not a Clay Institute award, nor has it received a Lean/Mathlib community joint certification equivalent to this week’s Riemann proof. The repository contains approximately 2,270 files and approximately 600,000 lines of Lean code; however, the key certificate is explicitly noted as “will be released soon” and is not in the repository. The interval arithmetic verification required for the certificate is also outside the formalization and likewise not included in this repository. The authors explicitly stated they used Claude, which does not equate to “Claude independently proved it,” much less that the grand prize has been claimed. This event must also not be conflated with another Navier–Stokes-related announcement published by OpenAI on 8 September 2026.
