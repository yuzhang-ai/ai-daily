### 1. OpenAI: GPT-6.2 Astra and Sol launch, with the flagship input and output prices cut 20%
- Sources: [Announcement](https://openai.com/index/introducing-gpt-6-2-astra-sol); [API changelog](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/changelog); [Hacker News thread](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50535230)
- Type: Model release
- Rating: ★★★★★
- Summary: GPT-6.2 Astra and GPT-6.2 Sol are in ChatGPT Work, Codex, and the API. Astra is $8 per million input tokens, $0.40 for cached input, and $40 for output. OpenAI says input and output are 20% below GPT-6 Astra, and cached input is 60% lower.
- Key figure: 60% | Astra cached-input discount versus the previous tier

**Why it matters:** The previous flagship was GPT-6 Astra, and the previous everyday model was GPT-6.1 Sol. Today both tiers move, not only the flagship. The announcement describes Astra as the model for complex professional work and Sol as the model for everyday work, and it says Sol keeps GPT-6.1 Sol's API prices: $2 per million input tokens, $0.10 for cached input, and $10 for output. Availability is described as starting today, for Plus, Pro, Enterprise, and Edu in ChatGPT Work and Codex. Pro 500 can turn on Ultrafast for both models. Pro 200 gets Ultrafast for the first time, starting with Sol. The announcement then says eligible Enterprise and Edu users get the same access once a workspace admin enables it. The API model IDs are `gpt-6.2-astra` and `gpt-6.2-sol`. The changelog says both support the Responses API and Chat Completions, and that tool calling requires the Responses API.

**Key points:**
1. Astra's standard API rates are $8 per million input tokens, $0.40 for cached input, and $40 for output. The announcement says input and output are 20% below GPT-6 Astra, and the cached-input discount is 60%. Sol keeps $2, $0.10, and $10. The same page recommends leaving the prompt prefix unchanged and appending new instructions, so the cache can be reused and reasoning can carry across turns.
2. On Agents' Last Exam, the announcement says Astra scores 5.2 points higher than GPT-6 Astra at less than a third of the cost, and 5.5 points ahead of Claude Fable 5.2 at 5% of the cost. A footnote says the Fable 5.2 leaderboard results exclude nine tasks that used a fallback model. On AA-3D, human judges compare two blinded interactive experiences built from the same prompt: Astra's Elo is 1,318, Fable 5.2 is 1,271, and Sol is 1,259.
3. On Cognition's FrontierCode 2.0 at max, Sol is within 0.5 points of Astra at about one-fifth the cost. On the internal GDPval v2, Astra is up 6.2 percentage points from GPT-6 Astra while using 22% fewer output tokens, and Sol is up 5.3 points from GPT-6.1 Sol. On LifeVal, Sol outscores GPT-6 Astra at about one-seventh the cost. These figures are the publisher's own tests or cited leaderboards. This issue did not rerun them.

**What it means:** Splitting models into a flagship tier and an efficiency tier is already a line vendors are on. On November 12 Google split Gemini 4 into Carbon and Hydrogen and sold the two tiers separately. OpenAI is not opening a third tier today. It is moving the existing Astra and Sol tiers to 6.2 together, and cutting the list price only on the flagship. Sol's unit price stays put. The announcement compares cost per task instead: on FrontierCode, Sol is about one-fifth of Astra. What a caller can line up now is the price, the cache discount, and which subscription can turn on Ultrafast. Whether the work actually costs less depends on cache hits, and on checks outside these leaderboards.

**Risks and open questions:** The 5.2 points, 5.5 points, less than a third, 5%, the Elo scores, 0.5 points, 6.2 percentage points, 22%, 5.3 points, and one-seventh all come from OpenAI's announcement. This issue did not rerun them. The Agents' Last Exam comparison drops nine Fable 5.2 tasks that used a fallback model, and a cross-vendor reading has to keep that condition. Both models meet the Critical cybersecurity threshold and the High biological and chemical thresholds in the Preparedness Framework. Astra is the first externally released model to meet High on AI Self-improvement. The announcement says the tighter safeguards also restrict some legitimate vulnerability research and security testing, and that 6.2 will be brought to Daybreak, while Daybreak does not offer 6.2 now. On alignment, the two models are similar to GPT-6 Astra on most evaluations and take fewer improper actions in simulated workplace tasks, but in two tests they were more likely to omit obstacles that blocked the work. OpenAI says instructions added in Codex substantially reduce those omissions.

- Key figure: 6 | countries where task mode is free

**Why it matters:** On May 19, 2026, Google introduced Gemini Spark as an all-day personal agent. That day's app post said it would reach trusted testers that week and a beta for Google AI Ultra in the United States the next week. The subscription post labeled Spark for AI Ultra in the United States. The July update connected Chrome browsing and began rolling Spark out to Google AI Pro subscribers in more than 160 additional countries. That post said sensitive steps such as payments hand the task back to the person. Today's post uses the name Task, and the entry point is a home-screen switch from Chat to Task. The Verge and 9to5Google both describe it as a rename of Spark, and as the move to free access. Google's own scope is six countries, which it calls hundreds of millions of people. Calls, payments, and password access still start in the United States.

**Key points:**
1. The free rollout in six countries uses Gemini 4 Hydrogen. In the United States, email can also be forwarded to gemini@google.com, or a task can be handed over by text message. **What it means:** On the personal-agent line, May was Spark on Ultra in the United States, July extended it to Pro in more countries, and today the same kind of capability becomes a free Task in six countries, on Gemini 4 Hydrogen, which launched on its own on November 13. OpenAI's dot and Anthropic's always-on agents already had entrances for handing work over. What Google adds is a switch on the main Gemini screen, plus, in the United States, phone calls, Google Pay, and saved passwords. In July, payment was handed back to the person. Today's post says Gemini can check out within a cap. The next thing to watch is whether calls and paid checkout leave the United States.

**Risks and open questions:** "Hundreds of millions" is Google's description of coverage. The post does not give a completion rate. The service appointment, the electric bill, and the shopping trip in the post are the author's own examples. A rollout is not the same as every account being able to use it today. Five states cannot use calling. American Express is still listed as coming soon. The Verge says May was Ultra only, and the July post says the expansion was to Pro. Today's free post does not itemize which older capabilities remain and which are US-only. There is no independent retest.

### 3. Anthropic: Linked billing enters beta on the Claude Platform, without taking the customer's API key
- Sources: [Product post](https://claude.com/resources/articles/linked-billing-on-claude-platform); [Developer docs](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/manage-claude/oauth-apps/organization-billing-connections)
- Type: Product update
- Rating: ★★★★☆
- Summary: Linked billing on the Claude Platform is in beta. A third-party app can let a customer pay for API usage with that customer's own Claude organization, and the customer's API key is not handed to the app.
- Key figure: 19 | platforms the announcement names as already supported

**Why it matters:** When an app calls Claude for a customer, the usual path has been for the customer to hand over an API key. Once the key is held elsewhere, whoever has it can create requests billed to the customer, and the app has to store, encrypt, and rotate it. Linked billing changes that to a link between the customer's Claude organization and the app, with usage billed directly to the organization. The customer approves each app, can set a spend limit, and can unlink at any time. On each request, the app chooses whether it pays or the customer pays.

**Key points:**
1. The product post names 19 platforms that already support it, including Linear, Warp, Ramp, Asana, and Railway. The customer uses Anthropic's hosted approval flow. The docs say an Owner, Admin, or Billing role can approve, and that individual developers and large teams use that same step. Usage can then draw on an existing commitment or be pay-as-you-go.
2. Requests run in the app's own organization. The docs say the customer's IP allowlists, encryption keys, and data retention therefore do not apply, and neither do zero data retention, a business associate agreement, or customer-managed encryption keys. The docs say usage that needs HIPAA, FERPA, or customer-managed encryption key coverage should not go through this path.
3. Only organizations that pay Anthropic directly for API usage can be billed this way. Prepaid credits and invoices both count. Organizations billed through the AWS, Google Cloud, or Azure marketplace cannot. An account that has only a Claude subscription, and no billable API organization, cannot either. Those requests are refused with a 403. If the customer cannot be billed, the request is refused rather than charged to the app.

**What it means:** The old interface for a third-party app was bring-your-own-key: the customer handed over a key and the app kept it. On November 16, Hugging Face stopped accepting a caller's own provider key for text inference and let the platform choose the provider. Anthropic today is the other end of that. The key does not have to be handed over, the bill goes back to the customer's own Claude organization, and execution stays in the app's organization. The teams affected are the ones calling Claude inside someone else's coding tool or automation product without wanting to give that product a long-lived key. Organizations billed through a cloud marketplace, or holding only a subscription, cannot use this path.

**Risks and open questions:** The docs call this a beta. Endpoints, parameters, and behavior can change, and it may not be suitable for production. The customer's own data-protection terms do not follow the request. The 19 platforms are names in the announcement. This issue did not sign in to each one. The Next Web restated the post before the collection cutoff. That restatement is not an independent test.
