### 1. Meta: Muse arrives in Instagram DMs and can organize saved posts
- Sources: [Official Instagram post](https://x.com/instagram/status/2125295853816692960); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/24/meta-brings-its-muse-ai-assistant-directly-into-instagram-dms/); [The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/tech/1041374/you-can-now-dm-your-muse-ai-agent-on-instagram)
- Type: Product update
- Rating: ★★★★★
- Summary: Instagram's official account said on November 24, 2026 that people can use Muse in DMs and ask it to organize saved content; TechCrunch reported that the feature is rolling out to iOS and Android users in the United States, Canada, Mexico, Japan, and Singapore.
- Image: ![Muse prompts and Collections in Instagram Saved](images/2026-11-26/2-muse-instagram-saves.png) (Image: Instagram)
- Key figure: 5 countries and regions | First mobile rollout scope reported by TechCrunch

**Why it matters:** Using Muse used to mean opening the standalone Muse app, or going through other entry points such as WhatsApp in markets where it was already available. Muse is now inside Instagram DMs: people can ask about a post, catch up on conversations they missed, organize saves into Collections or trip itineraries, and turn recipe Reels into grocery lists. A personal agent is no longer confined to its own app; it sits in a social feed where people already browse and save content. TechCrunch reported that Instagram conversations with Muse also appear as a side chat in the Muse app.

**Key points:**
1. Capabilities: Instagram's official post said Muse can organize saves into Collections or travel itineraries, find saved Reels, and turn recipes into grocery lists; people can also use it in DMs to learn more about a post or catch up on what they missed. The post also said Muse will not make changes or take action without approval.
2. Rollout: TechCrunch, at 9:00 a.m. Pacific Standard Time on November 24, 2026, reported that the feature began rolling out that day to iOS and Android users in the United States, Canada, Mexico, Japan, and Singapore; a Muse prompt that appears after saving a post is launching in the United States and Canada. The Verge later reported the same mobile rollout footprint and noted that it was citing TechCrunch.
3. Control: In addition to the confirmation described in the official post, TechCrunch quoted Meta as saying that nothing is shared, published, or sent without approval, and that people can ask Muse to forget information or undo actions.

**What it means:** Along the line of putting personal agents into existing super-apps, ChatGPT, Gemini, and Meta's own assistants have already tried conversational entry points. Muse had mainly lived in its standalone app and, in some regions, WhatsApp; what it is adding now is Instagram DMs, plus the ability to work directly with saved content. The judgment: for agents to enter daily life, the decisive step is not only a stronger model, but sitting next to a feed people already use. This is still a gradual rollout in a limited set of countries, and whether it becomes a default entry point will depend on coverage and on whether people actually want Muse to organize saves or plan trips for them.

**Risks and open questions:** The list of five countries and regions comes from TechCrunch; the official Instagram post does not name territories. The coverage describes a rollout starting, not a one-shot launch across those entire markets. According to TechCrunch, the post-save organization prompt is launching only in the United States and Canada, which is not the same footprint as the DM feature. What users actually see will also depend on account region, client version, and whether Muse is available to them.

### 2. Cognition: Releases coding model SWE-2.1, with Cyber and Ultrafast variants
- Sources: [Official Cognition blog](https://cognition.com/blog/swe-2-1); [Official Cognition post](https://x.com/cognition/status/2125305349414523061)
- Type: Model release
- Rating: ★★★★☆
- Summary: Cognition released coding model SWE-2.1 on November 24, 2026, saying it was trained with pure reinforcement learning on Kimi K3, and also introduced Cyber and Ultrafast variants; Desktop and CLI use is free through December 8.
- Image: ![Official benchmark table comparing SWE-2.1 with SWE-2 and other models](images/2026-11-26/1-swe-21-benchmarks.png) (Image: Cognition)
- Key figure: 10× | Official claimed generation speed of Ultrafast versus the standard version

**Why it matters:** The core models behind coding agents are shifting from general chat models toward specialized versions trained further on long-horizon engineering and security work. Cognition released SWE-2.1, saying it was trained with pure reinforcement learning on Kimi K3, as its predecessor was, and that it improves substantially on SWE-2 on benchmarks including FrontierCode 2.0 and Terminal-Bench 4.1. At the same time, the company released SWE-2.1 Cyber, a version with more permissive safeguards aimed at defensive work, and SWE-2.1 Ultrafast, offered with Cerebras and officially described as 10× faster. For Devin users, this is a new model they can switch to, not just another evaluation write-up.

**Key points:**
1. Benchmarks (vendor-reported): The comparison table in the official blog lists SWE-2.1 at 50.0 on Terminal-Bench 4.1 (24.2 for SWE-2), 41.3 on FrontierCode 2.0 (24.1 for SWE-2), and 80.0 on ExploitBench (36.1 for SWE-2). In the same table, Claude Opus 5.5 scores 65.8 on Terminal-Bench 4.1, 47.3 on FrontierCode 2.0, and 91 on ExploitBench, so SWE-2.1 is not first on those boards. The post asks readers to see the Benchmark Methodology Notes at the end.
2. Variants and safety: SWE-2.1 Cyber is the more permissively safeguarded version; the company said it will power regular scans in Devin Security, which already offers deep scans backed by models such as Mythos. The blog also said a canary evaluation found new sandbox-escape vulnerabilities four times during training, and that researchers were alerted and responded each time.
3. Availability: The official blog said SWE-2.1 is free through December 8 on Desktop and CLI, and available in Devin Desktop, Cloud, and CLI at a discounted price until January 30. The official @cognition account said at 19:07 UTC on November 24, 2026 that it is free for all Devin Pro, Max, and Teams users for the next two weeks.

**What it means:** On the line of callable coding models, the SWE series is the specialist model Cognition trains for Devin. SWE-2 already followed the recipe of pure reinforcement learning on Kimi K3; 2.1 is an upgrade on that same path, adding faster inference and a more permissive security variant. The judgment: specialist coding models are still using reinforcement learning to stretch long-horizon and security work, but the vendor's own numbers show it trailing general-purpose flagships such as Claude Opus 5.5 on several boards. For paying Devin users, the more immediate change is a free trial window, after which they can decide whether to keep using it during the discount period.

**Risks and open questions:** All benchmark figures are vendor-reported; the method is described in the notes at the end of the blog, and no independent reproduction has been seen. Some comparison models have no score on individual boards, and the accompanying image is a simplified official table — the full figures are in the blog post. The free and discounted periods have end dates, and the official post's "two weeks" needs to be read alongside the blog's December 8 and January 30 dates by plan and endpoint. The actual availability of the Cyber variant is determined by Devin Security.

### 3. PrismML: Open-weights Ternary Bonsai 2.5 27B, stronger on-device coding and tool use
- Sources: [Official PrismML announcement](https://prismml.com/news/prismml-launches-ternary-bonsai-2-5-27b); [9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2026/11/24/prismmls-bonsai-2-5-brings-better-coding-and-tool-use-to-on-device-ai/); [Hugging Face GGUF repo](https://huggingface.co/prism-ml/Ternary-Bonsai-2.5-27B-GGUF)
- Type: Model release
- Rating: ★★★★☆
- Summary: PrismML released Ternary Bonsai 2.5 27B on November 24, 2026, saying the model is based on Qwen 3.8 27B, occupies 5.9 GB, and is available for free download under the Apache 2.0 license.
- Key figure: 54.2 | Vendor-reported average on three coding benchmarks (37.9 for the previous generation)

**Why it matters:** After squeezing a model of roughly 27B scale onto a personal device, the harder problem is multi-step tool use: one mistake can send the later steps off course. PrismML released Ternary Bonsai 2.5 27B, describing it as an upgrade to Ternary Bonsai 2 27B, still based on Qwen 3.8 27B, still 5.9 GB in size, and available for free under Apache 2.0 from November 24, 2026. The company said it focused on longer agent tasks — how the model takes in tool output, recovers from failures, and avoids unproductive reasoning.

**Key points:**
1. Vendor-reported averages: On three coding benchmarks (SWE-bench Verified, DeepSWE v1.1, and Terminal-Bench 2.1), version 2.5 scored an average of 54.2, compared with 37.9 for the previous generation. On two tool-use benchmarks (AppWorld and τ³-Bench), the average was 82.2, compared with 60.2. Full per-benchmark scores and evaluation settings are in the official whitepaper.
2. Runtime: The company said the model uses ternary weights, runs on NVIDIA and Intel GPUs through llama.cpp, and runs on Mac, iPhone, and iPad through MLX.
3. How to get it: The official announcement said developers, researchers, and other users can download it for free starting November 24, 2026. The Hugging Face GGUF repository prism-ml/Ternary-Bonsai-2.5-27B-GGUF was created at 16:32 UTC on 2026-11-24.

**What it means:** On the line of coding models that can run on-device, PrismML has already shipped the Bonsai series of compressed models. Version 2.5 is still based on Alibaba's Qwen 3.8 27B; what it adds is longer-horizon tool use, not a smaller footprint. The judgment: when a local agent needs to edit private code, run tests, and revise from the results, whether a compressed model can stay stable across many tool calls matters more than another single-shot score. These figures remain vendor-reported and should be re-checked on one's own repos and hardware.

**Risks and open questions:** The benchmark figures are vendor-reported averages; per-benchmark results are in the whitepaper. 9to5Mac cited some individual scores that this item does not use, sticking instead to the averages in the official announcement. Actual speed, memory use, and the install size on Apple devices may vary with quantization and runtime. Being able to load the model on a phone is not the same as completing a full coding-agent workflow there.
