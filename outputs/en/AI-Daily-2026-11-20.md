### 1. Google: The Gemini Mac app can operate the machine in the background
- Sources: [Official blog](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/products/gemini-app/computer-use-mac/); [OpenAI computer use guide](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/tools-computer-use)
- Type: Product update
- Rating: ★★★★★
- Summary: Computer use is rolling out in the Gemini Mac app starting today, clicking through local apps and the web inside an isolated window. The scope is English in the United States. The footnote requires Apple Silicon, macOS Sequoia 15.0 or later, and a personal paid account for someone 18 or older.
- Image: ![A Gemini Mac chat sends a modeling request with @Computer use, and the isolated window has Blender open](images/2026-11-20/1-gemini-mac-computer-use.png) (Image: Google)
- Key figure: 15.0 | minimum macOS Sequoia version

**Why it matters:** The post's `datePublished` is 2026-11-19T17:00:00+00:00, 01:00 on November 20 in Beijing, and the date printed on the page is November 19 in the United States. Computer use on a desktop and in a browser already exists. OpenAI's computer use guide documents both a persistent desktop and a persistent browser: the desktop sample uses Python and PyAutoGUI, and the browser sample uses Playwright to run JavaScript. [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/19/google-gives-gemini-the-ability-to-use-your-mac-for-you/) writes that OpenAI, Anthropic, and Meta had already offered this kind of agent feature in their desktop apps. The November 12 issue recorded Antigravity browser use. On November 17, OpenAI opened a computer-use agent to healthcare customers. The November 18 issue recorded Gemini Tasks running on the cloud Agent Compute Engine and connecting apps on the account, not clicking a local interface. Today's item is the Gemini Mac app: eligible personal paid accounts, clicking local apps and the web in a separate background window, with the main screen and cursor left to the person.

**Key points:**
1. The post says the rollout starts today, in English, in the United States, and the download address is gemini.google/mac. Footnote 1 requires Apple Silicon, macOS Sequoia 15.0 or later, a personal Google account for someone 18 or older, and availability for Google AI Plus, Pro, and Ultra. That sentence and "English in the United States" are two separate limits.
2. Gemini works in an isolated background window, with a live preview in the chat. The post says the main screen and cursor stay free. The person chooses which apps it can open, can pause at any time, and key actions need approval first.
3. The examples on the page are batch color grading, filling shipping forms from local PDFs, building slides from dashboards, and turning clothing photos into resale listings. These are usage notes, not measured success rates.

**What it means:** On the computer-use line, models can already operate a desktop and a browser. OpenAI's guide puts the persistent desktop and the persistent browser on the same page. Tasks on November 18 connects cloud apps. What Google adds today is a background window in the consumer Mac app, with the main cursor left to the person who is using the computer. The people affected are in the United States, in English, on the paid personal accounts and Apple Silicon named in the footnote. The free tier, Intel Macs, and Windows are not in this post.

**Risks and open questions:** "Rolling out" does not mean every eligible account can use it the same day. The examples have no success rate. The isolated window, optional apps, live preview, pause, and approval of key actions are the product's control description. Those sentences are not virtual-machine hardware isolation, not inference that stays on the machine, not a promise that data stays on the machine, not arbitrary permission, and not a guarantee that payment cannot happen on its own. The machines and plans in the footnote, and English in the United States in the body, both have to be met.

### 2. OpenAI: GPT-Live-1.1 enters the API, and a WebRTC session can stand by
- Sources: [Changelog](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/changelog); [Model page](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/models/gpt-live-1.1); [Session guide](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/live-conversations); [October 15 post](https://x.com/OpenAI/status/2110797830361436604)
- Type: Model release
- Rating: ★★★★☆
- Summary: GPT-Live-1.1, already rolling out in the products on October 15 while the API was described as coming soon, is in today's API changelog. Active time is $0.05 per minute and paused WebRTC inference is $0.02 per minute. The prices are listed per minute and billed per second.
- Key figure: $0.02 | per minute while inference is paused; active is $0.05

**Why it matters:** The changelog places general availability of the GPT-Live-1 API on September 10. The official account's October 15 post has `created_at` 2026-10-15T18:19:54Z, 02:19:54 on October 16 in Beijing. That post says GPT-Live-1.1 is rolling out to all users, covering ChatGPT, Codex, and dots, and that the API was coming soon. Today's changelog records the model ID `gpt-live-1.1` under Nov 19, with no time zone and no clock time. At the collection cutoff that page did not give an exact first-release time. This briefing does not add a clock time, and it does not treat the whole calendar day of November 19 as inside the window. The same entry says WebRTC sessions for GPT-Live-1 and GPT-Live-1.1 can be configured for standby: inference pauses during inactivity and resumes when someone speaks. The billed state is whether inference is active or paused. Silence is not standby. The [pricing page](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/pricing#live-session-duration) lists both models at $0.05 per active minute and $0.02 per standby minute, and says voice sessions are billed per second. The GPT-Live-1 model page also says duration is not rounded up to the next whole minute. The lower standby rate applies to WebRTC sessions while inference is paused. Backend models and tools are billed separately. The [cost guide](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/voice-latency-cost) says silence before the idle timeout expires still counts as active, and muting the microphone leaves the session active.

**Key points:**
1. The model page says GPT-Live-1.1 can listen while it speaks. Compared with GPT-Live-1, it says transcription is more accurate and accent and voice stay more consistent across languages. When to hand a task to the backend for reasoning or tool use is described in plain language. Input is audio and text, and output is audio and text. The only supported endpoint is `v1/live/sessions`. Chat Completions, Responses, and Realtime are not supported.
2. The session guide says that setting `session.wake_policy.type` to `speech` when creating a WebRTC session waits for speech before inference starts. `session.idle_timeout_s` is an integer from 5 to 60, the number of seconds without an input or output transcript before inference pauses and the session enters standby. If that timeout is omitted, inference stays active after the first wake until the session ends. `vad_threshold` runs from 0 to 1 and defaults to 0.5. The microphone track stays open during standby, and the connection and session ID stay the same. Silence before that timeout still counts as active.
3. Standby is written for both GPT-Live-1 and 1.1. It is not the first release of 1.1, and it is not a price cut that belongs only to 1.1. Both model pages now list $0.05 and $0.02. The changelog does not say how far this pair of prices moved from an older price on any particular day.

**What it means:** On the full-duplex voice line, September 10 was the GPT-Live-1 API, and the post at 02:19:54 on October 16 in Beijing put 1.1 in the products while the API was described as coming soon. What the changelog adds is `gpt-live-1.1`, plus WebRTC standby shared by 1 and 1.1. The two rates split on whether inference is running or already paused. If nobody is speaking and the idle timeout has not expired, the session can still sit on the $0.05 rate. The people affected are applications already on the Live API. Omitting the timeout leaves inference active after the first wake, and that switch is easier than the model ID to leave the bill on the $0.05 rate.

**Risks and open questions:** "More accurate transcription" and "more consistent accent and voice" are statements on the model page. They have no scores, and this briefing did not rerun them. The changelog has only Nov 19, with no time zone and no clock time. An exact first-release time was not on the page at the cutoff. The standby rate is described for paused WebRTC inference. The prices are listed per minute and billed per second. Backend calls are not included in the duration price. The new voice languages in the October 15 post were that day's product rollout, not today's API change.
