### 1. Google: The Gemini Mac app can operate the machine in the background
- Sources: [Official blog](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/products/gemini-app/computer-use-mac/)
- Type: Product update
- Rating: ★★★★★
- Summary: Computer use is rolling out in the Gemini Mac app starting today, clicking through local apps and the web inside an isolated window. The scope is English in the United States. The footnote requires Apple Silicon, macOS Sequoia 15.0 or later, and a personal paid account for someone 18 or older.
- Image: ![A Gemini Mac chat sends a modeling request with @Computer use, and the isolated window has Blender open](images/2026-11-20/1-gemini-mac-computer-use.png) (Image: Google)
- Key figure: 15.0 | minimum macOS Sequoia version

**Why it matters:** The post's `datePublished` is 2026-11-19T17:00:00+00:00, 01:00 on November 20 in Beijing, and the date printed on the page is November 19 in the United States. Computer use had mostly stayed inside developer agents. The November 12 issue recorded Antigravity browser use: an agent opens pages, takes screenshots, and checks changes in the built-in browser. On November 17, OpenAI opened a computer-use agent to healthcare customers in ChatGPT Work and Codex. OpenAI's API docs now describe computer use as connecting to a browser or desktop runtime, and the sample on that page runs JavaScript in a persistent browser. What the Gemini Mac app adds today is another layer: the person keeps their own screen and cursor, and Gemini clicks in an isolated window beside them.

**Key points:**
1. The post says the rollout starts today, in English, in the United States, and the download address is gemini.google/mac. Footnote 1 requires Apple Silicon, macOS Sequoia 15.0 or later, a personal Google account for someone 18 or older, and availability for Google AI Plus, Pro, and Ultra. That sentence and "English in the United States" are two separate limits.
2. Gemini works in an isolated background window, with a live preview in the chat. The post says the main screen and cursor stay free. The person chooses which apps it can open, can pause at any time, and key actions need approval first.
3. The examples on the page are batch color grading, filling shipping forms from local PDFs, building slides from dashboards, and turning clothing photos into resale listings. These are usage notes, not measured success rates.

**What it means:** On the computer-use line, Antigravity on November 12 covers looking at pages in a browser, and the sample in OpenAI's docs also stays in a persistent browser. What Google adds today is an isolated window inside the consumer Mac app, with the main cursor left to the person who is using the computer. The next bar for a desktop agent is whether it blocks that person's own work, not only whether it can hit a button. The people affected are in the United States, in English, on the paid personal accounts and Apple Silicon named in the footnote. The free tier, Intel Macs, and Windows are not in this post.

**Risks and open questions:** "Rolling out" does not mean every eligible account can use it the same day. The examples have no success rate. The safety design is the vendor's description, with no independent audit. The machines and plans in the footnote, and English in the United States in the body, both have to be met.

### 2. OpenAI: GPT-Live-1.1 enters the API, and a WebRTC session can stand by
- Sources: [Changelog](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/changelog); [Model page](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/models/gpt-live-1.1); [Session guide](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/live-conversations); [October 15 post](https://x.com/OpenAI/status/2110797830361436604)
- Type: Model release
- Rating: ★★★★☆
- Summary: GPT-Live-1.1, already rolling out in ChatGPT, Codex, and dots on October 15, is in today's API changelog. The model page prices active time at $0.05 per minute and WebRTC standby at $0.02 per minute.
- Key figure: $0.02 | standby per minute; active is $0.05

**Why it matters:** The changelog places general availability of the GPT-Live-1 API on September 10. The official account's October 15 post has `created_at` 2026-10-15T18:19:54Z, 02:19 on October 16 in Beijing. That post says GPT-Live-1.1 is rolling out to all users, covering ChatGPT, Codex, and dots, and that the API was coming soon. Today's changelog records the model ID `gpt-live-1.1` on November 19, with no clock time. The same entry says WebRTC sessions for GPT-Live-1 and GPT-Live-1.1 can be configured for standby: inference pauses when nobody is speaking and resumes when someone speaks. The model page bills by duration, $0.05 per active minute and $0.02 per standby minute, and says the lower standby rate applies to WebRTC sessions while inference is paused. Backend models and tools are billed separately.

**Key points:**
1. The model page says GPT-Live-1.1 can listen while it speaks. Compared with GPT-Live-1, it says transcription is more accurate and accent and voice stay more consistent across languages. When to hand a task to the backend for reasoning or tool use is described in plain language. Input is audio and text, and output is audio and text. The only supported endpoint is `v1/live/sessions`. Chat Completions, Responses, and Realtime are not supported.
2. The session guide says that setting `session.wake_policy.type` to `speech` when creating a WebRTC session waits for speech before inference starts. `session.idle_timeout_s` is an integer from 5 to 60, the number of seconds without an input or output transcript before the session returns to standby. If that timeout is omitted, inference stays active after the first wake until the session ends. `vad_threshold` runs from 0 to 1 and defaults to 0.5. The microphone track stays open during standby, and the connection and session ID stay the same.
3. Standby is written for both GPT-Live-1 and 1.1. It is not a discount that belongs only to 1.1. Both model pages now list $0.05 and $0.02. The changelog does not say how far this pair of prices moved from an older price on any particular day.

**What it means:** On the full-duplex voice line, September 10 was the GPT-Live-1 API, and October 15 put 1.1 in the products while the API was described as coming soon. What arrives today is a callable `gpt-live-1.1`, plus two duration rates on WebRTC that split on whether anyone is speaking. If a call is billed at the active rate the whole time, waiting also costs money. Standby prices the waiting on its own. The people affected are applications already on the Live API. Omitting the timeout leaves inference active after the first wake, and that switch is easier than the model ID to leave the bill on the $0.05 rate.

**Risks and open questions:** "More accurate transcription" and "more consistent accent and voice" are statements on the model page. They have no scores, and this briefing did not rerun them. November 19 in the changelog has no clock time. Standby is described for WebRTC only. Backend calls are not included in the per-minute duration price. The new voice languages in the October 15 post were that day's product rollout, not today's API change.
