### 1. OpenAI: ChatGPT Wallet starts rolling out, and each purchase still needs approval
- Sources: [OpenAI post](https://openai.com/index/introducing-chatgpt-wallet)
- Type: Product update
- Rating: ★★★★☆
- Summary: ChatGPT Wallet is rolling out to ChatGPT Pro users in the US aged 18 and older. A dot can check out with a payment token issued by the card network after the user approves each purchase. The real card number is not given to the agent or the merchant.

**Why it matters:** Using ChatGPT to look up products, compare options, and decide what to buy is something OpenAI describes as already happening. OpenAI says 1.2 billion people use ChatGPT each week. Payment used to stay outside the chat. On November 12, Brave turned its Search API into pay-per-request: an agent settles in USDC on Base, no account required, and LLM Context plus web, news, image, video, and local search are all $5 per 1,000 requests. What OpenAI is shipping today is the other path. The person's card stays with the card network, the agent receives a payment token, and the user has to approve every purchase.

**Key points:**
1. The first group is ChatGPT Pro, age 18 or older, in the US, with an eligible US-issued card. The named partners are American Express, Mastercard, and Visa. The user adds a card once, reviews the items, total, and selected card, then passes a security check such as Face ID or a fingerprint. The payment network then issues a payment token with a card number, security code, and expiration, charged to the selected card. The real card details are not shared with the merchant or the agent.
2. The user keeps that card's existing rewards and its protections for eligible disputes, for example when an item is defective. The user still transacts with the business, which keeps pricing, fulfillment, returns, and customer service. OpenAI calls this the first wallet that lets an agent pay without access to the real card number and that requires a security check on every purchase.
3. The same post tells businesses what to do: claim the business in ChatGPT and upload product information; recognize signed requests with Web Bot Auth so a firewall does not block them by mistake; and work with Forter, Riskified, Sardine, and Signifyd, plus Adyen, Checkout.com, and Stripe, so risk systems recognize legitimate agent orders. Letting an agent buy on its own within a spending limit, a category, or a time window is described as a later expectation, not a feature available today.

**What it means:** Agent payments split into two shapes across two days. Brave's piece is buying search per request without an account. OpenAI's piece is checking out on an ordinary website without handing the real card number to the model or the merchant. Authorization for retail still sits with the user. The next place this is likely to stick is merchant risk systems declining legitimate orders. OpenAI itself describes sites blocking agents, and risk systems declining them, as the problem to work through now. A purchase the user does not approve one by one is not what this post says is available today.

**Risks and open questions:** This is a rollout to Pro users in the US, not general availability. "The first wallet of this kind" is OpenAI's wording. The weekly figure of 1.2 billion people is also from this post. Sites can still block agents, and risk systems can still decline the charge. The security check is illustrated with Face ID or a fingerprint, not a full device list.

### 2. Google: Intelligent eyewear opens for pre-order, on sale December 10
- Sources: [Google pre-order post](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/technology/xr-ar/google-intelligent-eyewear-collections-pre-order/); [May I/O preview](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/platforms/android/android-xr-io-2026/); [Meta AI glasses store](https://www.meta.com/ai-glasses/)
- Type: Product update
- Rating: ★★★★☆
- Summary: Intelligent eyewear from Google with Warby Parker, Gentle Monster, and Samsung is open for pre-order. Warby Parker starts at $395, Gentle Monster starts at $449, and both go on sale in stores and online on December 10.
- Image: ![Warby Parker and Gentle Monster frames, with a camera visible on the temple](images/2026-11-14/2-eyewear.jpg) (Image: Google, 不裁剪)
- Key figure: $395 | Warby Parker starting price

**Why it matters:** Glasses with a camera and a conversational assistant are already for sale. Meta's store currently lists Ray-Ban Meta and Oakley Meta, describes a camera and an AI you can talk to, and shows prices from $224. Google previewed its own line on May 19 at I/O: audio glasses would come first later this fall, and Gentle Monster and Warby Parker frames were already named, but there was no price and no way to order. Pre-orders open today, with December 10 as the on-sale date.

**Key points:**
1. The design partners are Warby Parker, Gentle Monster, and Samsung. A footnote on the post says intelligent eyewear is only available with Google accounts from the United States and South Korea, and it requires a compatible Google account that is 18 or older. That footnote lists supported phones as Pixel 7 series and above running the Android 17 September 2026 update or later; Galaxy S, Galaxy S FE, Galaxy Z Fold, Flip, Fold Ultra series running One UI 9.0 or higher; and iPhone 13 Pro and above. It does not name individual models past those series, so the compatibility range is the footnote on this post. Gentle Monster has six designs, prescription lenses for most frames, sales in the United States and South Korea, from $449. Warby Parker has three styles, Alden, Reid, and Wren, in optical or sun, from $395, with guided demos at more than 360 stores.
2. Google says the frames weigh around 41 grams, the battery lasts up to 9 hours, and the case can recharge the frames up to 7 times. The camera is a 12-megapixel ultra-wide. Say "Hey Gemini" or tap the frame. Google Maps gives turn-by-turn directions. Google Translate does live speech translation with automatic language detection across more than 70 languages.
3. Pre-orders are already live online and in store at Gentle Monster and Warby Parker. Hands-on demos are available at select stores and at the Google Store. Sales in stores and online start on December 10. Camera visuals used for Gemini are not saved in Gemini History. Google says those visuals are not used to train models or target ads, and that bystanders' faces are redacted first. A photo or video shows an indicator light, and recording stops if the light is covered or disabled. A hardware switch lets the wearer use them as ordinary glasses.

**What it means:** The May post split intelligent eyewear into audio glasses, which would ship first, and a second kind that shows information on the lens. Today's pre-order post describes open-ear speakers, a camera, and Gemini in the voice, and it does not describe a display on the lens. That matches the audio glasses from May. What Google adds is not the category of an assistant in a pair of glasses. It adds the price, the phone range in the footnote, including iPhone 13 Pro and above, and the date December 10. Meta's glasses can be bought now. Until December 10, Google's pair is a pre-order and an in-store demo.

**Risks and open questions:** Weight and battery life are Google's approximate figure and upper bound. The post does not put the test conditions in the same sentence. Gentle Monster is specified for the United States and South Korea. The post does not list Warby Parker's countries one by one. Use is limited to Google accounts from the United States and South Korea that are 18 or older, and to the phone series named in the post's footnote. The privacy design is the vendor's description, with no independent audit. The display glasses are not part of today's pre-order.

### 3. DeepSeek: The app's search, files, and voice move into the API, with tool prices
- Sources: [DeepSeek API news](https://api-docs.deepseek.com/news/news261113); [Chat app guide](https://api-docs.deepseek.com/guides/build_a_chatbot); [Frontend repo](https://github.com/deepseek-ai/chat-demo)
- Type: Product update
- Rating: ★★★★☆
- Summary: DeepSeek puts the app's search, link reading, files, safety checks, and voice on the deepseek-flash API. Search and fetch are each $0.75 per 1,000 calls, and a chat frontend with no app backend is public.
- Image: ![A documented chat screen marking thinking, web search, page reading, and an answer with citations](images/2026-11-14/3-deepseek-blocks.png) (Image: DeepSeek)
- Key figure: $0.75 | per 1,000 search or fetch calls

**Why it matters:** The DeepSeek app already had search, link reading, and voice. A developer who wants the same things in their own product has usually had to wire those services up separately. On November 12, Brave opened its Search API as pay-per-request at $5 per 1,000, selling Brave's own index. What DeepSeek attached today is not another standalone search engine. It is a set of tools on V4.1-Flash: search and fetch are each priced at $0.75 per 1,000 calls, and the tool fees sit on top of token charges.

**Key points:**
1. The model ID is deepseek-flash, for V4.1-Flash. Messages, Responses, and Chat Completions can all use these capabilities, with tool calls and context assembly done on the server. Built-in tools cover web or image search with citations when thinking is off; search and page reading during multi-step reasoning when thinking is on; parsing PDFs, Office files, and images into the conversation; automatically reading links in the user message; and checking inputs and outputs, returning a refusal marker when a risk is detected. Search, fetch, OCR, speech recognition, and speech synthesis can also be called on their own.
2. The pricing figure on the announcement says: search $0.75 per 1,000 requests, fetch $0.75 per 1,000 requests, OCR $0.225 per 1,000 pages. Speech recognition is $0.058 per hour, with $0.116 struck through and marked 50% off for a limited time. Speech synthesis is $0.225 per 10,000 characters, with $0.45 struck through and the same limited-time 50% off.
3. Public-facing apps must turn safety checks on. Developers remain responsible for content moderation, applicable model registration, and labeling AI-generated content. DeepSeek also published deepseek-ai/chat-demo: the browser calls the API directly, no application backend is required, and existing DeepSeek conversation history can be imported. The repository was created on 2026-11-12, the license is MIT, and the star count on 2026-11-14 was 686.

**What it means:** This collects capabilities that already lived in the app onto a developer price list. It is not a new foundation model. Brave sells standalone search, settled per request. DeepSeek sells search, page reading, and voice attached to its own model, and public apps are required to turn safety checks on. What a developer writes less of is the integration layer. What they do not avoid is the token fee, the question of when the voice discount ends, and the fact that once web search, link reading, a high safety check, or a title is enabled, the server sets parameters such as temperature itself and the values in the request no longer apply.

**Risks and open questions:** Tool prices come from the pricing figure on the announcement. Both voice rows are marked as a limited-time discount. The safety check is described as basic risk control. There is no independent retest of search or voice quality. The figure does not list the token price itself. The capabilities above are stated for deepseek-flash.
