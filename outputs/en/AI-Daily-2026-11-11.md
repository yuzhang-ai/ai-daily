## Top stories

### 1. Google: Gemini 4 Carbon is generally available; Antigravity previews multi-agent /swarm the same day
- Sources: [Google blog](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/gemini-models/gemini-4-carbon-now-available/); [Antigravity /swarm docs](https://antigravity.google/docs/swarm); [Hacker News discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50442303)
- Type: Model release
- Rating: ★★★★★
- Summary: Google opened its flagship model Gemini 4 Carbon to all paying developers and Google AI Ultra subscribers at an introductory price of $2 per million input tokens and $10 per million output tokens, doubling after November 30.
- Image: ![Gemini 4 Carbon key art](images/2026-11-11/1-gemini-4-carbon.jpg) (Image: Google)
- Key figure: $2 | per million input tokens at launch, doubling from Nov 30

**Why it matters:** Two weeks ago Carbon was only available to a small group of cybersecurity defenders through the Fairwind program; now it is in the AI Studio API, the Gemini app and AI Mode in Search. Google positions it above Gemini 4 Argon: most coding and knowledge work stays on Argon, while the hardest multi-step coding, debugging and research goes to Carbon, which spends more tokens exploring and verifying. /swarm, previewed the same day, turns one prompt into a group of agents working in parallel, with Carbon suited to the architecture, coordination and review roles.

**Key points:**
1. Google says Carbon ranks first on CursorBench 4.0, Perplexity's WANDR and Zapier's AutomationBench, at less than half the per-task cost of the next best external model.
2. Launch pricing matches Argon: $2 per million input tokens and $10 per million output tokens; from November 30, 2026 it rises to $4 and $20.
3. /swarm makes the current conversation the coordinator, splits the task across workers that run in parallel and collaborate on a shared message board, then has a verifier agent test the changes. It is a preview for Google AI Pro and Ultra users, in both the Antigravity app and the CLI.

**What it means:** The tiering of frontier models is getting clearer: a cheaper workhorse handles most tasks and a slower, more expensive model appears only at critical steps. /swarm turns that division of labour into a product feature, so the question for developers is no longer which model but which model at which step. The launch price lasts only 20 days; cost estimates built on Carbon should use the post-November 30 numbers.

**Risks and open questions:** The three benchmark rankings and the cost comparison all come from Google's launch post and have not been independently reproduced. /swarm is a preview, and the docs warn that every worker is a separate agent, so quota drains much faster than a single conversation.

### 2. Anthropic: Claude adds spreadsheets, whiteboards and data dashboards; partner apps can run inside the conversation
- Sources: [Claude blog](https://claude.com/resources/articles/more-ways-to-work-with-claude-sheets-whiteboards-and-interactive-connectors); [Sheets, Whiteboards and Data](https://claude.com/resources/articles/sheets-whiteboards-data); [Hacker News discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50443716)
- Type: Product update
- Rating: ★★★★★
- Summary: All paid Claude plans get Claude Sheets and Claude Whiteboards in beta, Claude Dashboards becomes generally available as Claude Data, and apps from partners such as Adobe and Atlassian can be operated directly inside Claude.
- Image: ![Claude Sheets and Whiteboards launch image](images/2026-11-11/2-claude-sheets.png) (Image: Anthropic)
- Key figure: 9,000+ | connectors and plugins on the Claude Marketplace

**Why it matters:** Planning or analysis done with Claude used to end up copied into another tool for collaboration, at which point Claude lost sight of later edits. Anthropic now makes spreadsheets, whiteboards and dashboards shared artifacts inside Claude that a team keeps editing together, exporting to partner tools when needed. Together with the upgraded interactive connectors, Claude is moving from an assistant that answers questions to a place where teams do the work.

**Key points:**
1. Claude Sheets generates spreadsheets with multiple tabs, formulas, formatting and dropdowns. Claude Whiteboards turns a meeting discussion into a timeline with owners and dates, then into a document or deck, or into Lucid, Miro or Zoho's Vani.
2. Claude Data connects to sources such as Snowflake and Salesforce, can use metric definitions certified by the data team (beta), and produces dashboards, visual data stories or narrated short videos.
3. Interactive connectors let partners embed their apps in Claude, starting with Adobe and Atlassian; plugins from Salesforce (beta), NetSuite and Workday (beta) ship with built-in skills. The Claude Marketplace already lists more than 9,000 connectors and plugins.

**What it means:** Spreadsheets, whiteboards and BI dashboards are among the most-used categories of office software, and Anthropic is walking straight into territory held by Microsoft, Google and every SaaS vendor. For SaaS companies, building a Claude connector starts to look like the mobile question of a decade ago: users may stop opening your app and use your features inside Claude instead.

**Risks and open questions:** Sheets and Whiteboards are still beta, as are the Salesforce and Workday plugins. Claude Meetings has been announced without a date. Team collaboration touches enterprise data permissions, so admin controls and data retention need checking before adoption.

### 3. Browser vendors: Modern Web Guidance 1.0 gives coding agents current web knowledge
- Sources: [Chrome for Developers blog](https://developer.chrome.com/blog/modern-web-guidance-v1); [GitHub repository](https://github.com/GoogleChrome/modern-web-guidance); [Hacker News discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50443812)
- Type: Open source
- Rating: ★★★★☆
- Summary: Modern Web Guidance, maintained jointly by the Chrome, Edge, Firefox and MDN teams, reached a stable 1.0 release; packaged as agent skills, it injects modern browser API usage and compatibility data into a coding agent's context.
- Image: ![Modern Web Guidance 1.0 image](images/2026-11-11/3-modern-web-guidance.png) (Image: Chrome for Developers)
- Key figure: 4 teams | Chrome, Edge, Firefox and MDN maintain it together

**Why it matters:** Training data contains far more old code than new, so coding agents often write heavy JavaScript for things browsers now do natively. Modern Web Guidance appeared as a preview at Google I/O in May; the 1.0 release is one expert-reviewed reference to modern practice backed by four web platform teams.

**Key points:**
1. 1.0 adds a modernization workflow that scans an existing codebase for legacy patterns that can safely move to newer web features: `npx modern-web-guidance@latest legacy-scan .`.
2. The guide library targets hundreds of verified AI knowledge gaps; every guide is signed off by the relevant area tech lead, and user-facing guides get a separate accessibility review.
3. All guides are evaluated, with a public dashboard comparing agents with and without the guidance.

**What it means:** The way to give an agent knowledge is shifting from waiting for the next model to installing a skill pack. When the platform owner writes the pack, the content tracks browser releases. Cloud, payments and database platforms are likely to follow with official agent skills for their own APIs.

**Risks and open questions:** The evaluation dashboard is run by the project itself and the gains have not been reproduced by third parties. The guides enter the agent's context and cost tokens; the team says they are trimmed based on the evals, but the real overhead in large projects needs measuring.

## Open source worth a look

1. Project: meta/astryx
   GitHub: [meta/astryx](https://github.com/meta/astryx)
   Stars: about 24k (2026-11-11)
   What it solves: Meta's open-source React design system, used internally for 8 years across more than 13,000 apps, with 150+ accessible components, theming and a CLI.
   Why it's worth a look: docs, API and CLI are designed as one reference for people and agents alike, a view of how a large design system prepares for coding agents. Currently beta.
2. Project: vercel-labs/agent-browser
   GitHub: [vercel-labs/agent-browser](https://github.com/vercel-labs/agent-browser)
   Stars: about 50k (2026-11-11)
   What it solves: a command-line browser written in Rust that lets an agent open pages, read content and click elements one command at a time.
   Why it's worth a look: `snapshot` renders the page as a compact accessibility tree where every element has a ref such as `@e1`, so the agent acts by ref instead of relying on screenshots.
3. Project: addyosmani/agent-skills
   GitHub: [addyosmani/agent-skills](https://github.com/addyosmani/agent-skills)
   Stars: about 116k (2026-11-11)
   What it solves: a set of engineering skills for AI coding agents covering spec, plan, build, test, review and ship.
   Why it's worth a look: senior-engineer workflows and quality gates expressed as nine slash commands such as `/spec`, `/test` and `/review`; a good study in how to write agent skills.
