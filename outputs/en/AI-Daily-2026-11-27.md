### 1. Google: Guided Vision in Gemini Live comes to iOS
- Sources: [Official Gemini account post](https://x.com/GeminiApp/status/2125685936344236471); [Digital Trends report](https://www.digitaltrends.com/phones/gemini-lives-guided-vision-now-works-on-iphone-but-google-left-out-a-few-details/); [Official Android blog](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/products/gemini-app/guided-vision-gemini-live/)
- Type: Product update
- Rating: ★★★★☆
- Summary: The official Gemini account said on 25 November 2026 that Guided Vision in Gemini Live is now available on iOS; users can open Live, point the camera, and get real-time guidance on screen.

**Why it matters:** Real-time visual assistance is not a new category. Be My Eyes, Microsoft Seeing AI, and visual aids in Apple’s system have already helped blind or low-vision users describe what is in front of them. In its [official blog](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/products/gemini-app/guided-vision-gemini-live/) on 1 October 2026, Google placed Guided Vision in the Android version of Gemini Live: after sharing the camera, users can hear descriptions of their surroundings and receive spoken reframing cues if the shot is too high, too close, or off to one side. Today’s news is that the same feature has reached iOS. The official post only says it is now available on iOS. Google did not publish a separate iOS blog post, and it did not list supported devices, system versions, regions, or languages.

**Key points:**
1. The official @GeminiApp post has `created_at` 2026-11-25T20:19:55Z, which is 04:19:55 on 26 November in Beijing time. The text says Guided Vision in Gemini Live is now available on iOS, and that users can open Live, point the camera, and get real-time guidance on screen.
2. The 1 October Android launch post says the feature was built with the blind and low-vision community. Users can share a camera in Gemini Live to receive dynamic audio descriptions and spoken reframing cues. Official examples include reading small print on food packages, making out washing-machine cycles, finding a dropped earbud, and matching clothes. The help documentation still mainly covers the Android setup steps and accessibility shortcuts.
3. Digital Trends reported the same iOS launch on 25 November 2026 and noted that the official announcement is brief and does not say whether availability is limited to certain countries or languages. The report is timestamped 9:13 PM and does not state a time zone.

**What it means:** On the line of real-time visual assistants on a phone, dedicated apps and system features already exist. Google first put Guided Vision into Gemini Live on Android in October, and today it is adding the iPhone. The industry takeaway: for this kind of feature to enter daily life, the test is whether it sits inside the camera and voice session people already use, rather than in a separate accessibility app. Google has not yet stated the iOS version, region, or language range, so the actual experience will depend on the account’s region, the client version, and whether the framing cues are reliable.

**Risks and open questions:** “Now available” does not mean every iPhone account can turn it on the same day. The official post does not list iOS versions, devices, regions, or language limits, and the help page was still Android-focused at the collection cutoff. The official Android blog states that the feature is not a medical device or a mobility aid, and should not be used for navigation, safe-travel guidance, or obstacle detection. This issue did not include an iOS device test.

### 2. OpenAI: ChatGPT and Codex will share a usage allowance
- Sources: [Official Help Center](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/20001625-understanding-chatgpt-and-codex-usage-limits); [The Verge report](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/1042187/openai-is-combining-chatgpt-and-codex-usage-limits)
- Type: Product update
- Rating: ★★★★☆
- Summary: OpenAI’s Help Center says that starting in December, the usage allowance on Plus, Pro, and Business plans will be shared across ChatGPT and Codex; Instant chat will not draw from that allowance, and a small group of users may see the change earlier.
- Key figure: 12 | month when Plus, Pro, and Business start sharing an allowance

**Why it matters:** ChatGPT and Codex previously had usage that could be counted separately inside the same plan. The official Help Center now says that, as OpenAI brings the agentic capabilities of Work into the main ChatGPT experience, the usage allowance on Plus, Pro, and Business plans will be shared across ChatGPT and Codex starting in December, and will be measured in tokens. The Verge reported the same change at 2026-11-25T16:39:06Z (00:39:06 on 26 November, Beijing time). For subscribers who use both chat and coding tools, this is a change in how usage is counted, not a new model.

**Key points:**
1. The Help Center says that starting in December, the usage allowance on Plus, Pro, and Business plans will be shared across ChatGPT and Codex, because OpenAI is bringing the agentic capabilities of Work into the main ChatGPT experience. Usage is measured in tokens; more advanced models and more complex tasks generally use more of the allowance.
2. Instant chat will not draw from that usage allowance. Users will be able to see combined remaining usage and reset times in Settings → Usage. If they reach the limit, they can wait for it to reset, upgrade their plan, or buy credits to continue using eligible features.
3. OpenAI says it expects most users will not need to wait for their usage to reset more often than they do now. A small group of users may see the usage changes before December so the company can learn from early feedback before expanding more broadly. The Verge restated the shared allowance and the December timing, and wrote that OpenAI is preparing to fold the chat and work tabs into one interface.

**What it means:** After putting chat and a coding agent in the same plan, the next question is whether the allowance stays split or is combined. OpenAI is choosing, from December, to merge ChatGPT and Codex usage on Plus, Pro, and Business, while stating that Instant chat will not count toward it. The industry takeaway: people who use both chat and coding will have to plan around a single token budget. OpenAI expects most people will not have to wait for resets more often, but the actual effect will only be visible in December.

**Risks and open questions:** The Help Center describes a December start, not a full rollout on the collection day; only a small group may see the change early. The page does not give a specific token allowance for each plan, and it does not list which features are “eligible” for continued use with credits. The statement that most users will not need to wait more often is an official expectation, not a guarantee. This issue could not open a contemporaneous TechCrunch article (it returned a Cloudflare challenge page) and therefore uses only the Help Center and The Verge wording that was checked.

### 3. Alibaba: Qwen-Audio-3.1 adds multilingual and Chinese dialect support
- Sources: [Official account post](https://x.com/Alibaba_Qwen/status/2125798914490249411); [ASR model page](https://www.qwencloud.com/models/qwen-audio-3.1-asr-flash); [TTS model page](https://www.qwencloud.com/models/qwen-audio-3.1-tts-flash); [Realtime model page](https://www.qwencloud.com/models/qwen-audio-3.1-realtime-flash)
- Type: Model release
- Rating: ★★★☆☆
- Summary: Alibaba’s official account @Alibaba_Qwen said on 26 November 2026 that the already released Qwen-Audio-3.1 series now has enhanced multilingual capabilities, with a focus on Chinese dialects; the post says ASR, TTS, and Realtime cover 59 languages plus 8 Chinese dialects, 65 plus 6, and 53 plus 6, respectively, and that the models are available on Qwen Cloud.
- Key figure: 59 languages | Official count for ASR, plus 8 Chinese dialects

**Why it matters:** Multilingual and dialect speech is not a capability that appeared today. OpenAI’s speech interfaces, open-source Whisper, and Chinese systems such as SenseVoice and CosyVoice have already worked on recognition or synthesis. The Qwen-Audio-3.1 series itself was released as a family of speech foundation models on 23 September 2026. What the official account announced today is a multilingual upgrade to that series, with a special emphasis on Chinese dialects. The model pages confirm that ASR, TTS, and Realtime all support multiple languages and dialects, but they do not repeat the language and dialect counts given in the official post.

**Key points:**
1. The official post has `created_at` 2026-11-26T03:48:51Z, which is 11:48:51 on 26 November in Beijing time, after the previous issue’s collection cutoff. The text says the Qwen-Audio-3.1 series of speech foundation models had already been released, and that today the company is bringing enhanced multilingual capabilities, with a focus on Chinese dialects.
2. The official post lists the coverage as: ASR, 59 languages and 8 Chinese dialects; TTS, 65 languages and 6 Chinese dialects; Realtime, 53 languages and 6 Chinese dialects. It also gives three Qwen Cloud endpoints: qwen-audio-3.1-asr-flash, qwen-audio-3.1-tts-flash, and qwen-audio-3.1-realtime-flash.
3. The Qwen Cloud model pages say ASR is aimed at short-form speech recognition and supports multilingual and regional Chinese dialect recognition; TTS is aimed at real-time interaction and supports multiple languages and dialects, plus controls such as emotion, tone, and speaking rate; Realtime is a full-duplex speech model that supports multilingual conversation and tool use. At the collection cutoff, those pages did not list language or dialect inventories that fully matched the official post.

**What it means:** On the speech-foundation-model line, multilingual and dialect coverage already exists across several products. Qwen-Audio-3.1 shipped as a series in September; today’s addition is an official multilingual upgrade, especially for Chinese dialects. The industry takeaway: whether dialects can be recognized and synthesized reliably will affect whether a voice assistant can enter local, everyday settings. The specific language and dialect counts currently rest mainly on the official account post, and they still disagree with figures on the project page, so the documentation should be checked again before use.

**Risks and open questions:** The figures of 59, 65, and 53 languages and 8, 6, and 6 Chinese dialects come from the official account post; the model pages do not itemize them. The project page at [qwenaudio.github.io](https://qwenaudio.github.io/qwen-audio-3.1-asr/) separately says ASR supports 30 languages and 16 Chinese dialects, which conflicts with the official post. This issue could not confirm that the two pages describe the same product surface, so it does not treat the project-page figures as the size of today’s upgrade. The official post does not name the eight dialects, and it does not provide independently checkable benchmark scores. This issue did not run a dialect recognition or synthesis test.
