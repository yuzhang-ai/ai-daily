### 1. Alibaba: Qwen4-Max reaches general availability, with fewer active parameters and a lower list price
- Sources: [Launch blog](https://qwen.ai/blog?id=qwen4-max); [Model documentation](https://platform.qianwenai.com/docs/developer-guides/getting-started/latest-model); [Qwen platform pricing](https://platform.qianwenai.com/docs/developer-guides/getting-started/pricing); [Model Studio pricing](https://www.alibabacloud.com/help/zh/model-studio/model-pricing)
- Type: Model release
- Rating: ★★★★★
- Summary: Qwen4-Max, previewed on Token Plan on November 11 without a public spec, is now generally available as model ID qwen4-max. The blog states a 2.4T backbone, 71B activated per token, and a separate 328B N-gram Embedding. The Singapore Model Studio price is below qwen3.8-max on the same page.
- Key figure: 71B | activated per token; 2.4T backbone

**Why it matters:** The November 11 preview did not publish parameter counts, context length, or a list price. Today's blog splits the architecture into three figures: a 2.4T backbone, 71B activated per token, and an additional 328B of N-gram Embedding parameters. That 328B is separate. It is not added to 2.4T and called a new total. The 71B is parameters activated per token, not memory, and not speed. Alibaba Cloud's press release uses a different sentence: 2.4 trillion total parameters, 71B activated per token, and about 25% fewer activated parameters than Qwen3.8-Max at the same 2.4T scale. Both pages state 2.4T and 71B. One says backbone plus a separate embedding. The other says total parameters. This issue does not add them together, and it does not turn the parameter count into memory or performance. The Model Studio help page was updated on 2026-11-16. In Singapore, qwen4-max is not tiered by input length: 1.50 dollars per million input tokens and 4.50 dollars per million output tokens. On the same table, qwen3.8-max is 2 dollars and 6 dollars. The Qwen platform pricing page states a Beijing deployment in yuan: 10 yuan input and 30 yuan output, with thinking and non-thinking at the same price. The US-dollar prices for the Singapore deployment and the Beijing yuan prices are not converted into each other.

**Key points:**
1. The model documentation states a context of 1,000,000 tokens. Non-thinking max output is 131,072. The combined cap on thinking plus the final answer is 262,144. The default thinking budget is 131,072. Inputs include text, images, and video, and the capability table also lists PDF. Thinking effort is low, high, or xhigh, and the default is high. That table says batch inference is unsupported, as are fine-tuning and deployment. Pay-as-you-go includes a free quota of 1 million tokens, separate from Token Plan Credits.
2. The blog says the cut in cached-input pricing is especially large, but it does not give Qwen3.8-Max's previous cache price. Qwen4's own rates can be checked. On the Qwen platform, explicit or implicit cache reads are 0.5 yuan, a 5-minute explicit cache creation is 12.5 yuan, and a 1-hour creation is 20 yuan, all per million tokens. Singapore Model Studio lists 0.075, 1.875, and 3 dollars. The documentation says implicit cache hits are not guaranteed. The new explicit cache works with Chat Completions, Responses, and Anthropic Messages. DashScope does not support it.
3. Work360 is Alibaba's own industry-task suite. The blog says it covers 26 top-level domains, 182 subdomains, and 721 specialized fields, and scores Qwen4-Max at 52.69 against 47.74 for Qwen3.8-Max. The same page says Work360 token use is about 24% lower at Xhigh and 35% lower at High. The next clause says Terminal-Bench 4.0 is about 48% and 49% lower, in the same order, Xhigh then High. Cost per Work360 task goes from 4.43 dollars to 1.67 dollars, which the blog calls a 62% reduction. The press release keeps the round figures only: about 35% fewer tokens at High effort, and about 60% lower cost per task. It does not contain 52.69. These figures are the publisher's own measurements or citations of third-party boards. This issue did not rerun them independently. Max weights are not open. The blog says smaller Qwen4 models will be open source. The Token Plan changelog records 2026-11-16 as general availability. The preview discount from the November 11 entry is not restated on the general-availability line.

**What it means:** The comparison point is Qwen3.8-Max, not a new total made by adding 2.4T and 328B. The blog puts the roughly 25% activation cut in the comparison with Qwen3.8-Max-Base. The press release puts the 25% cut at the same 2.4T scale, against Qwen3.8-Max. The Singapore Model Studio table puts the list price against the served qwen3.8-max model, from 2 dollars input and 6 dollars output per million tokens to 1.50 and 4.50. On November 11, Qwen4-Max entered Token Plan only as a preview. Today adds a production model ID, an activation count, a context length, and that lower list price. On November 12 Google split Gemini 4 into the flagship Carbon and the efficiency tier Hydrogen, sold as two products. Alibaba is replacing the Max tier in place. The weights stay closed. What a caller can line up today is price and context. Whether the bill actually falls depends on checks outside Work360, and on whether the cache hits.

**Risks and open questions:** The Work360 score of 52.69, the token reductions, and the 62% cost figure come from the rendered launch blog. The press release's "about 60%" and "about 35% fewer tokens at High" are round figures. They are not merged with 62% or 24% into a new number. The press release also says Qwen4-Max ranked 6th on Code Arena and names Agents' Last Exam, Vals Index, JobBench, and Chartography. These figures are the publisher's own measurements or citations of third-party boards. This issue did not rerun them independently. ### 2. Hugging Face: language-model inference now routes through OpenRouter, and bring-your-own provider keys no longer apply to text
- Sources: [Hugging Face blog](https://huggingface.co/blog/openrouter); [OpenRouter announcement](https://openrouter.ai/blog/announcements/hugging-face-inference-providers/)
- Type: Product update
- Rating: ★★★★★
- Summary: Hugging Face has switched Inference Providers routing for language models to OpenRouter. Existing code and tokens keep working. Text models can no longer be called with a caller's own provider key.
- Image: ![Intelligence index against cost per task for open-weight and proprietary models, index on the vertical axis and a log scale in dollars on the horizontal](images/2026-11-17/2-hf-openrouter-cost.png) (Image: Hugging Face)
- Key figure: 71.7% | open-weight share of OpenRouter tokens at the end of September

**Why it matters:** Previously a request picked one provider and returned that provider's error on failure. Language-model requests now go through OpenRouter, which chooses on live price, speed, and reliability, and retries another provider inside the same call. Image, video, and audio generation still go directly through Hugging Face, not through OpenRouter. Bring-your-own provider keys stop on the text path. The blog says custom keys still work for image and video providers. Audio generation stays on the old path. The blog does not say that custom keys for audio still work. OpenRouter says that in the October live experiment after the routing rebuild, capacity errors fell 46%, time to first token improved 16.9%, throughput rose 16.3%, and cost per request rose 6.0%. The Hugging Face post cites the first three figures and does not mention the 6.0%.

**Key points:**
1. A model ID can take a suffix: `:fastest`, `:cheapest`, or a provider name. A conversation stays on the provider that holds its prompt cache. To pin a session, send `X-HF-Session-Id`. Any provider can be turned off in settings. Hugging Face says pricing is unchanged and that it adds no markup on the provider price. PRO includes 2 dollars of credits a month. Team and Enterprise include 2 dollars per seat. Text models no longer accept a caller's own provider key. Custom keys for image and video still work. Audio is not in that custom-key sentence.
2. Hugging Face cites OpenRouter's October 2026 statistics: open-weight models went from 26.8% of tokens served in October 2025 to 71.7% at the end of September 2026, after a July peak of 77.5%. The same passage says the most-used model this week is DeepSeek V4.1 Flash 1017, at 59.2 trillion tokens. Average open-weight prices are about 0.28 dollars per million input tokens and 1.62 dollars per million output tokens, against 1.27 and 6.36 for closed models. On the chart, Kimi K3.1 Pro scores 46 on the intelligence index and Claude Fable 5.2 scores 65, at 3.68 dollars per task against 26.54. The caption says this is Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index v5.0.1, read on 2026-11-16, at each model's highest reasoning setting.
3. OpenRouter's announcement says 160 models from 38 labs are callable through this integration today: models that have a Hub page and that OpenRouter serves. It also says each request's trace can be sent to a Hugging Face Storage Bucket the caller owns. That feature is in beta. The file contains the input, the output, token counts, and cost, and leaves out images and other media. Hugging Face lists "connect an OpenRouter account" and "route more tasks through OpenRouter" as next steps. It does not say the trace export is on by default.

**What it means:** Multi-provider routing is what OpenRouter already did. Hugging Face writes that OpenRouter serves more than 800 trillion tokens a month, across 600-plus models and 80-plus providers. The Hub used to run its own router, so the weight page and the call path were two layers. Today the default call for chat models joins that layer, and the bill stays on the Hugging Face account. The callers affected are those who attached their own provider key to language models: that path is closed for text. Custom keys remain for image and video. Audio generation does not move to OpenRouter, and the blog does not say whether an audio custom key remains. Open-weight models are already the majority of tokens on this router, and the default button on the Hub follows that usage. Callers can still constrain the destination with a suffix or by turning a provider off, but a failure no longer stops at the first provider.

**Risks and open questions:** The 46%, 16.9%, 16.3%, and 6.0% figures are OpenRouter's October experiment, not a new Hugging Face measurement from today. The intelligence index and the cost per task come from Artificial Analysis and were plotted by Hugging Face. This issue did not rerun them. 71.7% is the end-of-September figure. OpenRouter's announcement separately says open-weight models were about 70% of paid tokens in October. The two numbers should not be merged. "A better provider" means the mix of price, speed, and reliability, not a quality ranking. The Hugging Face post and the OpenRouter post are the two parties to the deal, so they do not count as independent heat. Other reports inside the window are listed in the triage log.

### 3. Google: Ask Maps keeps a reopenable itinerary, with appointment lookup in the US first
- Sources: [Ask Maps update](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/maps/ask-maps-holiday-travel-features-2026/); [March 12, 2026 launch note](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/maps/ask-maps-immersive-navigation/); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/16/google-maps-can-now-build-trip-itineraries-with-real-time-details-about-each-stop/); [9to5Google](https://9to5google.com/2026/11/16/google-ask-maps-itineraries/)
- Type: Product update
- Rating: ★★★★☆
- Summary: Starting today, Ask Maps is rolling out reopenable day-by-day itineraries with numbered pins on Android and iOS. Desktop can export them to Google Docs. List search uses live availability. Appointment lookup and calling launch in the US first.
- Key figure: 30 countries | Europe expansion; itineraries follow where Ask Maps is already offered

**Why it matters:** Ask Maps launched in the US and India on March 12, 2026. It already answered multi-stop trips. The official example asks for stops on the way to the Grand Canyon, Horseshoe Bend, and Coral Pink Sand Dunes, and the answer includes directions, ETAs, and tips. The same post already covered restaurant reservations, saving places to a list, sharing them, and navigation. What is new today is narrower: an itinerary that can be opened again, arranged by day, with a numbered pin for each stop; a desktop export to Google Docs; search across saved lists against live availability; and, in the US, lookup of open slots for beauty, pet care, auto repair, and home repair. The blog says it can call those businesses. Live traffic and open status at departure, plus the "Know before you go" notes on parking, charging, weather, delays, and tolls, are grouped with appointment lookup and launch in the US first.

**Key points:**
1. Itineraries and list search are available everywhere Ask Maps is already offered. Pre-departure tips and appointment lookup launch in the US first. Other regions are described as later. Multi-stop directions, reservations, save and share, and navigation were already in the March post. They are not new today.
2. The same day, Ask Maps starts expanding to 30 countries in Europe. The blog names Spain, Germany, and the UK, and says this brings Ask Maps to more than 200 countries and territories. The rollout starts today. It is not switched on for every device at once.
3. The official example for list search asks which saved places can seat five people within 30 minutes. The appointment example is a nail appointment nearby at 4 p.m. Both sentences are examples in the product post. The blog does not give a success rate.

**What it means:** March's Ask Maps already turned a multi-stop question into directions, a reservation, a list, and navigation. It did not stop at one sentence and a map. Today the same entry point keeps an itinerary that can be opened again, desktop adds an export to Docs, and list search checks live availability. The US goes one step further by looking up service appointments and calling the business. That is different from a text itinerary written in a separate chat window: the numbered pins, open status, and navigation stay inside Maps. The piece to watch next is whether appointment lookup and the pre-departure tips leave the US. Itineraries and list search are already tied to wherever Ask Maps is offered.

**Risks and open questions:** "Starts rolling out" does not mean every account can use it today. The blog does not publish a success rate for calling businesses, or for what it calls the vibe of the next stop. The features shipped on a holiday schedule. The regional split above still holds: itineraries and list search follow where Ask Maps is offered, and pre-departure tips plus appointment lookup start in the US. There is no independent rerun.
