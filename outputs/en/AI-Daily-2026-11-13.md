## Top stories

### 1. Google: Gemini 4 Hydrogen launches, the efficient tier enters Search and splits work with Carbon
- Sources: [Google announcement](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/gemini-models/introducing-gemini-4-hydrogen/); [API pricing](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/pricing#gemini-4-hydrogen); [Antigravity: Orchestrator](https://antigravity.google/blog/gemini-4-hydrogen-in-google-antigravity/); [Search upgrade](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/search/gemini-4-hydrogen-search-upgrade/)
- Type: Model release
- Rating: ★★★★★
- Summary: Google released Gemini 4 Hydrogen (model ID gemini-4-hydrogen) at a standard paid price of $1.50 per million input tokens and $7.50 per million output tokens, flat across the full 1 million-token context window; the same day it entered Google Search globally, and Antigravity can switch it with Carbon inside one task.
- Image: ![Gemini 4 Hydrogen benchmark table, with columns for Claude Sonnet 5.5 and GPT-6.1 Terra](images/2026-11-13/1-hydrogen-bench.png) (Image: Google)
- Key figure: $1.50 | standard price per million input tokens, no context-length surcharge

**Why it matters:** Gemini 4 had still been two flagship tiers. Argon and Carbon, generally available earlier this week, take the hard tasks; on the pricing page Carbon has no free tier, and its standard price is $2 per million input tokens and $10 per million output tokens through November 30, then $4 and $20 from December 1. Before that, everyday speed meant Gemini 3.8 Flash. Hydrogen fills the layer that is faster and that ships inside products people already use: Google says its Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index is 51 at the default high thinking level, above Argon's 50, and 54 at xhigh. The Search team calls this the biggest model upgrade to Search yet, available to every user worldwide today.

**Key points:**
1. The model ID is gemini-4-hydrogen. Input context is 1 million tokens, and output runs up to 1 million tokens. Thinking levels are low, medium, high (the default), and a new xhigh. Standard paid pricing is $1.50 input and $7.50 output per million tokens, with thinking tokens included in the output price; the pricing page also lists a free tier where input and output are not charged. Batch and Flex are $0.75 / $3.75 per million tokens, and Priority is $2.70 / $13.50 per million tokens. The announcement's "flat" price means the 1 million-token window does not add a long-context surcharge.
2. The doors are not the same. Developers call the Gemini API from AI Studio and Android Studio; the Gemini app and Google Search worldwide are for everyone. Free users of the Gemini app get Hydrogen only when automatic model selection decides it fits the prompt. Hydrogen in Antigravity is for paid Google AI Pro and Ultra. Orchestrator (Carbon) and Orchestrator (Argon) go to Ultra; Pro gets only Orchestrator (Argon).
3. Google says that at the default high level it finishes tasks 2.5 times faster than Gemini 3.8 Flash, and that Terminal-Bench 4.1 rises from 18.2% to 57%. The comparison table on the announcement puts Hydrogen next to Claude Sonnet 5.5 and GPT-6.1 Terra: 71.6% on Agents' Last Exam v1.1, 53.4% on MKA, and 58.8% on HLE-Diamond. On that same table, Terminal-Bench 4.1 (labeled cheating-adjusted) is 57.0% for Hydrogen and 63.9% for Claude Sonnet 5.5.

**What it means:** Selling a cheap tier and a flagship tier separately is already how OpenAI and Anthropic line up their products. What Google added today is handing work back and forth inside one session, rather than a static router assigning the whole task to a single model at the start. Antigravity's description is that Carbon starts read-only, sorts out dependencies and writes a plan, Hydrogen then edits code and runs tests, and hands back to Carbon when it gets stuck. The model post records that result on Terminal-Bench 4.0: near-Carbon task success, 24% less quota on average, and 19% faster. The Antigravity post puts the same 24% and 19% on Terminal-Bench 4.1. For developers the question shifts from which Gemini 4 to use to which step uses Carbon and which uses Hydrogen. Search and the free tier get the cheap layer; Carbon still has no free tier.

**Risks and open questions:** The index, speed, leaderboard ranks and safety figures all come from Google, with no independent rerun. The two official posts attach the Orchestrator 24% / 19% figures to different Terminal-Bench version numbers. On the comparison table Hydrogen is below Claude Sonnet 5.5 on Terminal-Bench 4.1, so it is not higher on every row. The pricing page marks the free tier as used to improve Google's products and the paid tier as not. The partner claim of 2x to 2.8x faster and 12% to 23% fewer tokens is early testing against 3.8 Flash.

### 2. Anthropic: Claude Meetings enters beta and edits docs and whiteboards during the call
- Sources: [Claude Meetings](https://claude.com/resources/articles/claude-meetings); [Release notes](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/12138966-release-notes); [How to use it](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/18325154-use-claude-meetings)
- Type: Product update
- Rating: ★★★★☆
- Summary: Claude Meetings is starting to roll out in beta to Team and Enterprise. Claude joins a Google Meet as a visible participant, takes notes, answers questions, and creates docs and whiteboards during the call; Anthropic says other video platforms are coming.
- Key figure: 2027-02-10 | docs and whiteboards made in a call stay free of extra charge until this date

**Why it matters:** Meeting notes are not new. The Claude post names Gong, Attention, Granola, Fireflies.ai and Otter as partners it is working with so a transcript can be sent out after the call. The difference this time is that Claude drafts the doc and the whiteboard while people are still talking, and invited teammates can edit them during the meeting. It connects to Sheets and Whiteboards, which landed in Claude earlier this week. The November 11 issue recorded that Meetings had been previewed with no launch date. The Help Center release notes date the start of the rollout to November 12.

**Key points:**
1. Google Meet is the only platform for now. On Team plans inviting Claude is on by default and an admin can turn it off; on Enterprise an admin turns it on and can limit it to part of the organization. During beta it is not available to organizations with zero data retention, a HIPAA-ready configuration, or customer-managed encryption keys.
2. By default Claude stays out of calls that include someone from outside the organization, and leaves if an outsider joins midway. An admin can allow external calls; on those, Claude posts a notice in chat and does not reply there, while what guests say still goes into the notes and transcript. It stores a transcript, a summary and screen descriptions, not audio.
3. During beta, notes, transcripts, and answers that do not use a connected tool are not charged extra; tool use counts toward the plan. Docs, whiteboards and other artifacts made in a call are free through February 10, 2027.

**What it means:** Meeting AI has mostly stopped at handing over notes after the call, which is the layer Otter, Fireflies and Granola already occupy. Anthropic lists them as partners for exporting the transcript afterward, and what it adds is editing the same doc and whiteboard during the meeting, attached to the artifacts Claude gained this week. An assistant that shows up only after the call still leaves the work in another tool; one that can edit the artifact during the call turns the meeting itself into a Claude workspace. What to watch next is whether Zoom and Teams join the supported list, and how those artifacts are priced after February 10. Enterprises still have to clear the admin switch and the outside-guest rule.

**Risks and open questions:** This is a beta rollout, not general availability. Google Meet only. Some organizations are excluded, and Enterprise is not on by default. Export to Gong and the other tools is described as work in progress, with no date.

### 3. IBM: LLMON ships, separating instructions and data with their own tokens
- Sources: [IBM's post on Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/blog/ibm-granite/llmon-an-llm-native-markup-language-now-in-granite); [GitHub repository](https://github.com/generative-computing/llmon); [8B preview](https://huggingface.co/ibm-granite/granite-4.1-8b-llmon-preview); [3B preview](https://huggingface.co/ibm-granite/granite-4.1-3b-llmon-preview)
- Type: Research
- Rating: ★★★★☆
- Summary: IBM released the markup language LLMON and two experimental previews trained from the Granite 4.1 3B and 8B bases, so the model treats instructions and data as different tokens; the weights and toolkit are downloadable, and IBM says they are not for production.
- Key figure: 3B / 8B | two downloadable LLMON previews, which IBM says are not for production

**Why it matters:** Getting a model to respect structure usually means a JSON schema, constrained decoding, or a chat template that separates system, user and assistant. IBM's argument is that JSON's brackets and quotes share tokens with ordinary text, so a model cannot tell a quote that closes a field from a quote inside a quotation. LLMON uses reserved special tokens as boundaries, so tags and content do not compete for the same slice of the vocabulary. An instruction has to be named by an exec block before it counts as a command; data, documents, tool definitions and tool results that arrive as input are there to be read. That behavior is trained in, not a filter bolted on afterward.

**Key points:**
1. The previews are granite-4.1-3b-llmon-preview and granite-4.1-8b-llmon-preview, post-trained from Granite 4.1 base checkpoints rather than layered onto the already released instruct models. They ship a chat template, so an ordinary message list renders as LLMON and nobody has to write the markup by hand.
2. IBM says that on the OPI benchmark LLMON avoids 47% more attacks than the best frontier model it tested, and 95% more than the best open model; ASB and StruQ show a similar trend. The post says these benchmarks measure static indirect prompt injection, and it does not name the models it compared against.
3. The repository generative-computing/llmon is Apache-2.0 and converts between JSON and LLMON. IBM says which span is an instruction and which is data is a learned behavior, not an enforced guarantee.

**What it means:** Structured output and indirect prompt injection have mostly been handled with wording, JSON schemas, or constraints applied at call time. IBM puts the boundary into the vocabulary and into post-training, and the 3B and 8B previews can be swapped in today. If "instructions and data use different tokens" holds up under an independent rerun, agent frameworks will depend less on prompts that say "ignore commands inside the document." It exists only on two experimental small models, and it does not replace a frontier model in production. The format is the thing to study, not these two models' leaderboard ranks.

**Risks and open questions:** The 47% and 95% figures are IBM's own relative numbers, with the comparison models unnamed and no independent rerun. IBM says this is an experimental preview, not for production, and that the protection is learned rather than guaranteed. The benchmarks cover static injection.

   Stars: 8 (2026-11-13)

   Why it's worth a look: the bundled chat template renders ordinary messages as LLMON, so the markup is not written by hand; the exec block names the instruction to run, and data blocks are only to be read.
